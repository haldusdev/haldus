## Key Question 1: What is the complete set of structural kinds an entity can be?

### Takeaway
Across Coad, Fowler, DDD, Arlow/Neustadt, MDM and Kimball the same roughly 15 structural shapes recur: independent thing (master), type/description, reference code, role, owned detail, value object, link, business event/document, immutable event/ledger entry, derived projection/balance, hierarchy node, polymorphic subtype, extensible (custom-attribute) entity, process instance, and configuration. The current catalog covers about half; the clearest holes are value object/embedded, type-instance description, role, hierarchy node, polymorphic subtype, custom fields, ledger/posting, derived projection, reservation and numbering sequence.

### Cited Findings

#### Coad / Lefebvre / De Luca, "Java Modeling in Color with UML" (1999), chapter 1
- [V] An archetype is defined as a form or template for one of a small number of class categories, specifying the attributes, links, methods, plug-in points and interactions typical of classes in that category; classes follow it only "more or less", which is why the authors explicitly reject implementing an archetype as a superclass (inheritance is too rigid) and instead tag the class (a UML stereotype or a `@archetype` doc comment). — [Coad ch.1, Northeastern-hosted PDF](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] The four archetypes, in order of importance: (1) **moment-interval** (pink): something that must be worked with and tracked for business or legal reasons and that occurs at a moment or over an interval; examples: sale (moment), rental (checkout to return), reservation (from made until used, cancelled or expired); (2) **role** (yellow): a way of participation by a party (person or organization), place or thing; (3) **description** (blue): a "catalog-entry-like" description, a collection of values that apply again and again, plus behavior across all the things matching it; example: vehicle description (manufacturer, model number, available colors) vs. the individual vehicle with its own serial number, purchase date, odometer; (4) **party, place or thing** (green): someone or something that plays different roles. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] Typical attributes per archetype (Fig. 1-6): description knows type, description, item number, default value(s); party/place/thing knows serial number, name, address, custom value(s); role knows assigned number and status; moment-interval knows number, date/date-time/interval, priority, total, status; moment-interval detail knows quantity. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Moment-interval details (mi-detail)**: a moment-interval often has parts, "a little piece" it needs to do its job; modeled as MomentInterval 1 — 1..* MIDetail; the moment-interval has `addDetail` and `calcTotal` (computed by interacting with its parts), each detail has `qty` and its own `calcTotal`. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] Typical moment-interval methods (Fig. 1-7): makeMomentInterval (the business-process method), addDetail, calcTotal, recalcTotal, **complete**, **cancel**, and inter-moment-interval methods mi_generateNext, mi_assessWRTPrior, mi_assessWRTNext, mi_comparePlanVsActual. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Plug-in points**: a description gets an interface plug-in (IAssess) when it has algorithmically complex behavior that may need swapping; a moment-interval gets one (IMakeMomentInterval, e.g. IMakeSale with a "one click" or "shopping cart" variant) when the business process is expected to change. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Classification checklist (ordered, first match wins)**: is it a moment or interval the system must track for business/legal reasons -> pink; otherwise a role -> yellow; otherwise a catalog-like description (grouping of values applied again and again) -> blue; otherwise party/place/thing -> green (the default). — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf); same ordering in [Wikipedia: Object Modeling in Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_Modeling_in_Color) [S]
- [V] **Domain-Neutral Component**: the four archetypes plug together in one repeatable shape (Description -> Party/Place/Thing -> Role -> MomentInterval -> MIDetail, with PriorMI/NextMI on either side of the moment-interval); the authors state that all 61 domain-specific components in the book follow it. Tailoring rule: drop a class along the chain when its level of specificity is not needed; include blue when there are groupings of values that apply again and again, green when things must be tracked individually rather than by quantity, yellow when there are role-specific responsibilities, pink when the moment/interval must be remembered. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] Thing-to-description default inheritance: a party/place/thing has `getCustomElseDefaultValue` — it returns its own custom value or else asks its description for the default. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)

#### Martin Fowler — Analysis Patterns supplement, "Further Patterns" (eaaDev), PoEAA catalog
- [V] **Party** is the supertype of person and organization (so addresses, phones and relationships can attach to either); Fowler calls it a classic "unnamed concept" seen in data models as person/org, player, legal entity. — [Fowler, Accountability chapter (apsupp)](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf)
- [V] **Knowledge Level**: a group of objects that describe how another group of objects should behave (a.k.a. meta level), allowing the system to be altered without programming; conventionally connected to the operational level through a "thing" / "thing type" association, the thing type being the *type object* of the thing. — [Fowler, Accountability chapter](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf)
- [V] Fowler's online "Further Enterprise Application Architecture" catalog groups patterns as: Temporal (Audit Log, Time Point, Effectivity, Temporal Property, Temporal Object, Snapshot); Events (Domain Event, Event Collaboration, Event Sourcing, Agreement Dispatcher, Parallel Model, Retroactive Event); Accounting (Account, Accounting Entry, Accounting Transaction, Replacement Adjustment, Reversal Adjustment, Difference Adjustment); Base (Range); plus unclassified items including Money Bag and Proposed Object. — [martinfowler.com/eaaDev](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/)
- [V] Accounting structure: **Account** is a "pot" holding a list of entries and a balance, mainly a descriptor for classifying; **Accounting Entry** records one change to an account; **Accounting Transaction** links the entries of one transfer (optional if transfer tracking is not needed); the **balance is derived** as the sum of entries (for a period: entries within that period). — [Fowler, Accounting Narrative](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingNarrative.html)
- [V] Entry mutability is rule-governed, not absolute: entries may be immutable at all times, or mutable until they are booked. — [Fowler, Accounting Entry](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingEntry.html)
- [V] **Event Sourcing**: capture all changes to application state as a sequence of events; application state is purely derivable from the event log and can therefore be cached anywhere; snapshots avoid replaying from scratch. — [Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)
- [V] PoEAA object-relational structural patterns (each is a distinct structural shape): Identity Field, Foreign Key Mapping, **Association Table Mapping** (association stored as its own table), **Dependent Mapping** (one class performs the mapping for a child class), **Embedded Value** (an object mapped into several fields of another object's table), Serialized LOB (object graph in one field), **Single Table / Class Table / Concrete Table Inheritance**. Base patterns include **Value Object** (small object such as money or a date range whose equality is not identity-based), **Money**, Special Case. — [Fowler, PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/)

#### Arlow & Neustadt, "Enterprise Patterns and MDA" (2003), chapter 1 (publisher sample chapter)
- [V] A business archetype is a primordial thing that occurs consistently and universally in business domains and business software; a business archetype pattern is a collaboration between business archetypes with the same universality. Example given: Party = an identifiable, addressable unit that may have a legal status (usually a person or organization). — [Arlow & Neustadt ch.1, Pearson sample](https://www.pearsonhighered.com/assets/samplechapter/0/3/2/1/032111230X.pdf)
- [V] Structural lesson 1 — **optionality at model level**: every archetype in a pattern is optional, and features can be stereotyped `«o»` meaning the attribute/operation/relationship may be omitted entirely from the generated class (explicitly contrasted with a nullable `[0..1]` attribute whose slot still exists). Example: Payment has optional dateMade / dateReceived / dateDue / dateCleared depending on whether the system makes or accepts payments. This makes patterns *configurable* ("pattern configuration", intended to be automated by MDA tools). — [Arlow & Neustadt ch.1](https://www.pearsonhighered.com/assets/samplechapter/0/3/2/1/032111230X.pdf)
- [V] Structural lesson 2 — **pleomorphism**: a pattern may take a different structure (different archetypes, features, relationships) to fit a business context while keeping core semantics; example: an IdenticalProduct archetype pattern is a `«pleomorph»` variation of the Product archetype pattern. — [Arlow & Neustadt ch.1](https://www.pearsonhighered.com/assets/samplechapter/0/3/2/1/032111230X.pdf)
- [V] The sample chapter confirms Party (ch. 4) and Money (ch. 11, including Payment, PaymentMethod, currency with optional introductionDate/expirationDate) as archetype patterns. — [Arlow & Neustadt ch.1](https://www.pearsonhighered.com/assets/samplechapter/0/3/2/1/032111230X.pdf)

#### Silverston; Hay
- [S] Silverston & Agnew, "The Data Model Resource Book, Vol. 3: Universal Patterns for Data Modeling" (Wiley 2009) organizes its patterns by *structural concern* rather than industry: declarative roles ("what parties do", with Level 1 / Level 2 ... variants of each pattern, i.e. the same pattern at increasing generalization), status patterns (chapter 6, templates for modeling statuses of any entity), plus chapters on classification/hierarchy/contextual roles/rules per the table of contents; subtypes/supertypes are a base convention. — [O'Reilly listing and TOC](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-data-model/9780470178454/)
- [S] David Hay, "Data Model Patterns: Conventions of Thought" (Dorset House 1996): chapters The Enterprise and Its World (Parties, Employee Assignments, Organizations), The Things of the Enterprise, Procedures and Activities, Contracts (purchase/sales orders, contract roles, deliveries), Accounting, The Laboratory, Material Requirements Planning, Process Manufacturing, Documents, Lower-Level Conventions (ends with "The Universal Data Model"). — [O'Reilly listing](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/data-model-patterns/9780133488654/); [TDAN review](https://tdan.com/?p=5561)

#### Domain-Driven Design (Evans' DDD Reference; Fowler; Vernon)
- [V] **Entity**: an object distinguished by identity rather than attributes, a thread of continuity through a lifecycle; the identifier may come from outside or be system-generated. — [Evans, DDD Reference 2015](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] **Value Object**: when only attributes and logic matter; treated as immutable, no identity, side-effect-free operations. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] **Domain Event**: a full domain object representing something that happened; ordinarily immutable; typically carries the time it occurred plus the identities of involved entities, and often a *separate* timestamp for when it was entered into the system and by whom. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] **Service**: a significant process or transformation that is not the natural responsibility of an entity or value object is modeled as a standalone operation. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] **Aggregate**: cluster of entities and value objects with one root entity; external objects may hold references to the root only; invariants are defined for the aggregate as a whole and enforced by the root; the same boundary governs transactions and distribution (synchronous consistency inside, asynchronous across). — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] **Repository**: provided only for aggregate roots that need direct (global) access; add/remove and criteria-based selection. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] Fowler's restatement: an aggregate is a cluster of domain objects treated as a single unit (canonical example: an order and its line items); outside references go only to the root; transactions should not cross aggregate boundaries; aggregates are the unit of loading and saving. — [Fowler, DDD_Aggregate](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DDD_Aggregate.html)
- [S] Vernon's four aggregate rules of thumb (chapter headings of "Implementing Domain-Driven Design" ch. 10): model true invariants in consistency boundaries; design small aggregates; reference other aggregates by identity; use eventual consistency outside the boundary. — [InformIT chapter excerpt](https://www.informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2020371); [Vernon, Effective Aggregate Design](https://www.dddcommunity.org/library/vernon_2011/)
- [V] **CQRS**: a different model may be used to read information than to update it; fits with event sourcing and raises the likelihood of eventual consistency; Fowler cautions that for most systems it adds risky complexity and should be applied only to specific parts of a system. — [Fowler, CQRS](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/CQRS.html)

#### MDM taxonomy and Kimball
- [S] Malcolm Chisholm's layered taxonomy: metadata (table/column definitions, stable for the life of the database) -> reference data (code tables / domain values / valid values) -> enterprise structure data -> transaction structure data (the direct participants that must exist before a transaction fires) -> transaction activity data -> transaction audit data (tracks each transaction from initiation to termination); "master data" = reference + enterprise structure + transaction structure data. — [Chisholm, SupplyChainBrain 2007](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/185-master-data-whatever-that-is-has-to-be-properly-managed) (page returned 403 to direct fetch; content taken from search digest)
- [S] Kimball: all fact tables fall into three grains — transaction (one row per event, e.g. per order line), periodic snapshot (state captured at regular intervals, e.g. end-of-day stock), accumulating snapshot (one row per process instance with a defined start/end, *updated* as milestones are reached); factless fact tables carry no measures and model many-to-many relationships or event occurrences. — [Kimball Group, Fact Tables](https://www.kimballgroup.com/2008/11/fact-tables/); [Wikipedia: Fact table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact_table)

#### Inheritance, polymorphism, extension attributes
- [V] Three canonical inheritance mappings: single table (one table, columns for all classes' fields), class table (one table per class), concrete table (one table per concrete class). — [Fowler, PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/)
- [V] Rails documents the same shapes as first-class association kinds: Single Table Inheritance (shared table + `type` column; drawback: table bloat), **delegated types** (shared attributes in a superclass table, each subtype in its own table), **polymorphic associations** (a model belongs to several model types via an `_id` + `_type` column pair). — [Rails Guides, Association Basics](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/association_basics.html)
- [S] **Entity-Attribute-Value**: one row per fact (entity, attribute, value), for sparse/ad hoc attributes that cannot be fixed in advance (clinical findings, product catalogs with type-specific attributes); drawbacks: no DB-level typing/constraints, metadata tables to maintain, pivoting, slower queries; alternatives: JSONB columns (PostgreSQL 9.4+), SQL Server sparse columns, XML columns. Recommended only for the subsystem that actually needs the flexibility. — [Wikipedia: EAV model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity%E2%80%93attribute%E2%80%93value_model)
- [V] Coad's party/place/thing archetype carries `customValue` attribute(s) as a standard feature, i.e. instance-level extension values are part of the green archetype. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)

### Inferences
- Coad's ordered checklist ("first matching question wins, green is the default") is the only scheme found that guarantees *exactly one* archetype per class. A framework that wants "any entity maps to exactly one archetype plus traits" needs the same device: an ordered decision list with a default (Crud), not a set of independent predicates.
- Coad and Arlow both insist an archetype is "more or less followed" / has optional features. That is precisely the archetype + trait split: the archetype fixes the core shape, traits (and Arlow-style `«o»` features) are the omissible parts. Arlow's distinction between a *truly optional* member (not generated at all) and a *nullable* member is directly relevant to a source generator.
- Mapping of the Domain-Neutral Component onto the catalog: green thing -> Crud; blue description -> Lookup when it is only code+name, otherwise a Crud that is the *type* of other entities (the type-instance link is not expressible today); yellow role -> no equivalent; pink moment-interval -> Workflow/Crud/AppendOnly depending on mutability; mi-detail -> Detail.
- The catalog mixes two axes that the literature keeps apart: (a) *structural kind* (what the thing is: master, type, role, detail, link, event, projection) and (b) *lifecycle kind* (how it changes: mutable, immutable, state machine, versioned). Singleton/Store/ReadOnly/AppendOnly/Workflow/Job are lifecycle or cardinality variations; Lookup/Association/Detail are structural. A principled catalog would let structural kind select the archetype and lifecycle be either part of the archetype or a trait.
- Structural kinds with no home in the catalog (see table 1): value object/embedded; type/description with defaults; role; hierarchy node; polymorphic subtype family; custom/extension attributes; ledger entry with balanced transaction; derived projection/balance/snapshot; reservation/hold; numbering sequence; business document (header + lines + posting) as a distinct shape from generic Workflow.
- Action and Job correspond to DDD "Service" (an operation that "isn't a thing") and to process/accumulating-snapshot records respectively; they are operations/process instances rather than entities, which is consistent with keeping them as separate archetypes.

## Key Question 2: What is the complete set of relationship kinds?

### Takeaway
The literature distinguishes at least: reference by identity, composition/ownership, embedded value, plain many-to-many link, association with attributes (often typed and time-bounded), type-instance, role-of, generalization, polymorphic reference, self-reference (tree) vs. general graph, predecessor/successor between business events, plan/actual, temporal succession of versions, and derivation. The catalog expresses only ownership (Detail), scoped child (Under<Parent>), M:N link (Association) and tenant scoping (MultiTenant).

### Cited Findings
- [V] **Reference by identity vs. ownership**: only the aggregate root may be referenced from outside; internal members are reachable only through the root. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf); [Fowler, DDD_Aggregate](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DDD_Aggregate.html). [S] Vernon adds "reference other aggregates by identity". — [InformIT](https://www.informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2020371)
- [V] **Composition (dependent child)** at mapping level: Dependent Mapping — one class performs the database mapping for a child class; Rails' `dependent: :destroy` destroys children with the parent. — [PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/); [Rails Guides](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/association_basics.html)
- [V] **Embedded value**: an object mapped into several fields of its owner's table (no table, no identity). — [PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/)
- [V] **Association table** (pure M:N) vs. **association with its own attributes**: Rails `has_and_belongs_to_many` is a join table with no model; `has_many :through` uses a join *model* that can carry extra attributes, validations and callbacks. — [Rails Guides](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/association_basics.html); [PoEAA: Association Table Mapping](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/)
- [V] **Typed, time-bounded relationship (Accountability)**: when parties have several different kinds of links (matrix organizations), Fowler replaces multiple hierarchies with Accountability — a first-class relationship object between two parties (parent/child ends) with an Accountability Type; the sketch attaches a Time Period to the accountability; a knowledge level of Party Types and connection rules (allowed parent/child types) validates which parties may be connected. Summary intent: represents a complex graph of relationships between parties. — [Fowler, Accountability chapter](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf)
- [V] **Hierarchy (self-reference)**: Organization Hierarchy = recursive association with a `{hierarchy}` constraint (single parent, no cycles; cycle check on assignment); two parallel hierarchies (e.g. functional and regional parent) are acceptable, beyond that use Accountability; Fowler warns that giving up the single parent loses the ability to aggregate up the tree (his "Aggregating Attribute": a child inherits a parent's value). — [Fowler, Accountability chapter](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf)
- [V] Storage of hierarchies — **materialized path**: PostgreSQL `ltree` stores a label path (labels separated by dots, e.g. Top.Countries.Europe.Russia), with ancestor/descendant operators `@>` / `<@`, pattern matching (`lquery`), and B-tree/GiST index support. — [PostgreSQL docs: ltree](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ltree.html)
- [V] SQL Server `hierarchyid` (a compact path encoding): comparison is depth-first order; a sibling can always be generated to the left, right or *between* two siblings (i.e. it natively encodes **ordered siblings**); limitations: the column does not by itself guarantee a tree, uniqueness and referential integrity are the application's job; trade-offs vs. parent/child (adjacency list): subtree queries much faster with hierarchyid, moving a non-leaf subtree touches n rows instead of one, parent/child is better when queries rarely span the hierarchy or subtrees move often. Listed examples of hierarchical data: org structure, file system, project tasks, taxonomy of terms, graph of links between web pages. — [Microsoft Learn: Hierarchical Data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/hierarchical-data-sql-server)
- [S] Karwin ("SQL Antipatterns", "Naive Trees" chapter) names four tree models: adjacency list (each row references its parent; good for direct parent/children and inserts, poor for deep queries without recursive SQL), path enumeration, nested sets, closure table; each makes some operations easier at the cost of others. — [O'Reilly: SQL Antipatterns](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/sql-antipatterns/9781680500073/); [Karwin slides: Models for Hierarchical Data](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/models-for-hierarchical-data/4179181)
- [V] Rails "self joins" document the adjacency-list form (employee -> manager who is also an employee). — [Rails Guides](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/association_basics.html)
- [V] **Type-instance**: Coad's description (blue) to thing (green) link, one description to many things, with defaults flowing from description to thing; Fowler's thing / thing-type link between operational and knowledge level. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf); [Fowler, Accountability chapter](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf); Wikipedia states the blue-green relationship is type-instance — [Object Modeling in Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_Modeling_in_Color) [S]
- [V] **Role-of**: the role player (party/place/thing) holds core attributes valid regardless of the "hats" worn and enforces rules *across* its roles (example: `authorizedFor` on Person iterating its Cashier/Owner roles); each role then links to the moment-intervals it participates in. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Generalization** with three table mappings; **polymorphic reference** via type + id pair. — [PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/); [Rails Guides](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/association_basics.html)
- [V] **Predecessor/successor between business events**: the Domain-Neutral Component puts PriorMI and NextMI on either side of every moment-interval, with methods mi_generateNext / mi_assessWRTPrior / mi_assessWRTNext; Coad's example chain: request -> RFQ -> PO -> delivery -> invoice. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [S] SAP SD "document flow": viewed from a sales order, the quotation is a *preceding* document and delivery and invoice are *subsequent* documents; links are stored per item in a dedicated flow table (VBFA); "copy control" configures which data is copied from the source (preceding) to the target (subsequent) document and whether the flow is updated. — [SAP Help: Document Flow](https://help.sap.com/saphelp_470/helpdata/en/93/744f86546011d1a7020000e829fd11/content.htm); [SAP Learning: Copying Control](https://learning.sap.com/courses/fundamental-customizing-in-sap-s-4hana-sales/analyzing-the-copying-control-for-sales-documents)
- [V] **Plan/actual link**: a moment-interval may link 0..1 plan to 0..* actual moment-intervals, with mi_comparePlanVsActual. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Temporal succession (version chain)**: Fowler's Temporal Object — a continuity object (e.g. a contract) holding a collection of versions, so callers can refer either to the unchanging thing or to one specific version. — [Fowler, Patterns for things that change with time](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/timeNarrative.html). [V] Kimball type 2/6/7 dimensions use a per-version surrogate key plus a durable key shared by all versions. — [Kimball Design Tip 152](https://www.kimballgroup.com/2013/02/design-tip-152-slowly-changing-dimension-types-0-4-5-6-7/)
- [V] **Derivation**: balance derived from entries; application state derived from the event log; a read model separate from the write model. — [Fowler, Accounting Narrative](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingNarrative.html); [Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html); [Fowler, CQRS](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/CQRS.html)
- [S] **Temporal referential integrity** (a child's validity period must be covered by the parent's) and period-inclusive keys (`WITHOUT OVERLAPS`) are part of SQL:2011 for application-time tables. — [Wikipedia: SQL:2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL:2011)

### Inferences
- Detail vs. Under<Parent> in the catalog matches the DDD distinction exactly: Detail = member inside the parent's aggregate boundary (no own repository/endpoint root, modified only through the parent, deleted with it); Under<Parent> = a separate aggregate that merely carries a mandatory parent reference in its route/scope. The literature supports keeping both and never merging them.
- "Association" should be split in documentation into (a) pure link (existence only), (b) link with payload, (c) typed + effective-dated link (Fowler's Accountability; Arlow's PartyRelationship by name). (c) is expressible as Association + a missing effective-dating trait + a type reference.
- Self-reference is not just "a nullable FK to self": every source attaches rules (single parent, no cycles, subtree queries, move-subtree, ordered siblings). That is trait-shaped (e.g. `Hierarchical` / `Tree`) with a storage strategy option (adjacency list, materialized path/ltree/hierarchyid, closure table, nested set).
- Predecessor/successor ("generate next from this one, copy lines, keep a back-link") appears independently in Coad, SAP and Frappe (`amended_from`, see KQ3). It is a relationship kind with generated behavior (a "create X from Y" action that copies header+details and records the link) and is absent from the catalog.
- Type-instance differs from a Lookup reference in one rule the sources agree on: the instance inherits defaults from its type (Coad's getCustomElseDefaultValue) and the type constrains/validates the instance (Fowler's knowledge level). A Lookup reference only labels.
- A "snapshot copy" relationship (copy values from the referenced master at the time of the event, e.g. unit price onto an order line, so later master edits do not rewrite history) is implied by SAP copy control and by Kimball's type-2 reasoning, but no source read here names it as a pattern.

### Gaps
- Bill-of-materials / recursive composition (a self-referencing M:N with quantity, i.e. a DAG rather than a tree) was not verified from a primary source; Hay has a "Material Requirements Planning" chapter (TOC only). Fowler's remark that general graphs lose roll-up ability is the only verified statement about multi-parent structures.
- Nested set and closure table mechanics (left/right numbering; ancestor-descendant pair table with depth) are from background knowledge; only their names and the trade-off principle are sourced (Karwin, secondary).
- Exclusive arc and Karwin's critique of polymorphic associations (no FK enforceable on a type+id pair) — unverified in this session.
- Fowler's "Dealing with Roles" paper (role subtype, role object, relationship as role) was not fetched.

## Key Question 3: What is the complete set of lifecycle kinds?

### Takeaway
Independent sources converge on a small set of change regimes: overwrite in place; insert-only (optionally with compensating reversal); status-driven state machine (with complete/cancel as the minimal form); draft -> submitted/posted -> cancelled -> amended documents; approval by a second person; versioning on one or both time axes (record/system time vs. actual/application time); expiry; reserve -> confirm/cancel; long-running process with milestones; and derived (rebuildable) state. The catalog lacks effective dating/bitemporality, reversal/correction of immutable records, the document posting lifecycle, approval, reservation, gap-free numbering and derived state.

### Cited Findings

#### Mutable vs. immutable; corrections
- [V] Kimball's SCD types are the most explicit taxonomy of "what happens on change": **Type 0** retain original (value never changes); **Type 1** overwrite; **Type 2** add a row (new version); **Type 3** add a column (keep previous value alongside current); **Type 4** split volatile attributes into a mini-dimension; **Type 5** = 4 + type-1 outrigger to the current profile; **Type 6** = type-2 rows that also carry type-1 "current" attributes overwritten on all prior rows of the durable key; **Type 7** = dual keys (surrogate key to the type-2 row and durable key to the current row), same capability as 6 without overwriting. — [Kimball Design Tip 152](https://www.kimballgroup.com/2013/02/design-tip-152-slowly-changing-dimension-types-0-4-5-6-7/)
- [V] Domain events are ordinarily immutable because they record the past. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] Three ways to correct already-posted accounting results (driven by a Retroactive Event): **Replacement Adjustment** (delete wrong transactions, create correct ones; simple but history is lost), **Reversal Adjustment** (keep the wrong ones, post an opposite entry for each, then post the correct ones; keeps history, many cancelling entries), **Difference Adjustment** (keep the wrong ones and post only the difference; keeps history with fewer entries, more complex). — [Fowler, Accounting Narrative](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingNarrative.html)
- [V] Entries may be immutable always, or mutable until booked ("freeze on booking"). — [Fowler, Accounting Entry](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingEntry.html)
- [V] In Event Sourcing, events ideally can reverse themselves; this is simplest when events record differences rather than absolute values; external-system calls must go through gateways that can be disabled during replay. — [Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)

#### Temporal lifecycles
- [V] Fowler's temporal patterns: **Audit Log** (simple record of changes, cheap to write, awkward to query — for when you rarely go back); **Effectivity** (object explicitly marked with the period it is valid for; every client must handle the dates); **Temporal Property** (accessor takes a date; history hidden behind the property; good for a few properties); **Temporal Object** (object with explicit versions, each change creating a new version); **Snapshot** (a view of an object as of one date); **Time Point** as the index. Two dimensions: **actual time** (when it happened; Snodgrass: valid time) and **record time** (when the system learned it; Snodgrass: transaction time); bi-temporal = both. Fowler argues record-time history only needs "current" updates (appended now), since retroactive corrections are expressed in actual time. — [Fowler, Patterns for things that change with time](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/timeNarrative.html)
- [S] SQL:2011: a period is two ordinary columns with closed-open semantics; **application-time period tables** (`PERIOD FOR <name>`, a.k.a. valid time) where updates/deletes automatically split periods, with period-inclusive primary keys (`WITHOUT OVERLAPS`) and temporal referential integrity; **system-versioned tables** (`PERIOD FOR SYSTEM_TIME` + `WITH SYSTEM VERSIONING`) maintained by the system with constraints enforced on current rows only; **bitemporal** = both on one table. Support noted: DB2 10, Oracle 12c, SQL Server 2016 (system versioning), MariaDB 10.3 (system) / 10.4.3 (application time), SAP HANA 2.0; PostgreSQL listed as needing an extension. — [Wikipedia: SQL:2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL:2011)
- [V] SQL Server system-versioned temporal tables: two `datetime2` period columns managed exclusively by the engine plus a mirrored history table; INSERT opens a row (end = 9999-12-31), UPDATE and DELETE move the previous version to history and close its period at the transaction begin time; queried with `FOR SYSTEM_TIME` (AS OF, FROM..TO, BETWEEN..AND, CONTAINED IN, ALL). Stated use cases: auditing/forensics, reconstructing past state, trends, maintaining a slowly changing dimension, recovering from accidental changes. The page documents system time only (no application-time period support described). — [Microsoft Learn: Temporal tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/tables/temporal-tables)

#### State machines, documents, approval
- [V] Minimal status lifecycle in Coad: every moment-interval and every role carries a `status`; a moment-interval accepts `complete` and `cancel`; a reservation lives until it is used, cancelled or *expires*. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [S] Silverston vol. 3 devotes a chapter to status patterns as templates applicable to any entity. — [O'Reilly TOC](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-data-model/9780470178454/)
- [S] Frappe/ERPNext `docstatus` (a widely deployed generic document lifecycle): 0 = Draft (freely editable), 1 = Submitted (locked), 2 = Cancelled (permanently read-only). Allowed transitions: draft->draft (save), draft->submitted (submit), submitted->submitted (restricted "update after submit" fields), submitted->cancelled (cancel); a draft can be discarded straight to cancelled. A cancelled document can only be **amended**: a new draft is copied from it and linked via `amended_from`. Cancellation is blocked while other active documents link to it; a submitted document cannot link to a cancelled one. Only DocTypes flagged `is_submittable` get this lifecycle; most use only state 0. — [Frappe Framework docs: docstatus](https://docs.frappe.io/framework/doctypes/docstatus) (digest of the docs page; direct fetch of an alternate URL failed with HTTP 500)
- [S] **Maker-checker (4-eyes)**: every transaction needs at least two people — one creates it, a second confirms/authorizes it; rooted in segregation of duties; standard in banking systems. — [Wikipedia: Maker-checker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maker-checker)
- [S] Kimball accumulating snapshot: one row per process instance, inserted at the start and updated as each milestone is reached — a data-warehouse rendering of a multi-step lifecycle. — [Kimball Group, Fact Tables](https://www.kimballgroup.com/2008/11/fact-tables/)

#### Reservation, numbering, idempotency
- [S] **Try-Confirm/Cancel (TCC, "tentative operation")**: resources are held in a reserved state until confirmed or cancelled; cancel releases them; each reservation typically has a time limit after which the participant may cancel on its own; common HTTP mapping POST = reserve, PUT = confirm, DELETE = cancel; confirm and cancel should be idempotent. — [Oracle MicroTx docs: TCC](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/transaction-manager-for-microservices/24.4/tmmdg/tcc-transaction-model.html); [Enterprise Integration Patterns: Try-Confirm-Cancel](https://www.enterpriseintegrationpatterns.com/patterns/conversation/TryConfirmCancel.html)
- [V] **Gap-free numbering cannot come from a database sequence**: a `nextval` value is not reclaimed if the transaction aborts, gaps also arise from crashes and from `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT`, and the PostgreSQL manual states sequence objects cannot be used to obtain gapless sequences. — [PostgreSQL docs: Sequence functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-sequence.html)
- [V] Optimistic Offline Lock (detect a conflict and roll back) is Fowler's name for the concurrency regime behind version-stamped updates. — [PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/)
- [V] Domain-event identity for de-duplication: an event's identity can be based on its properties so that two arrivals of the same event are recognized as the same. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)

#### Derived state
- [V] Balance = sum of entries; application state = fold of events, cacheable anywhere, snapshot-able at any time. — [Fowler, Accounting Narrative](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingNarrative.html); [Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)
- [S] Periodic snapshot fact: state captured at fixed intervals instead of every movement (end-of-day stock level). — [Microsoft Learn: Design fact tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/training/modules/design-dimensional-models-fabric/3-design-fact-tables); [Kimball Group](https://www.kimballgroup.com/2008/11/fact-tables/)

### Inferences
- The catalog's `Revisioned` and `Auditable` both sit on the *record-time* axis (SCD type 2 / system-versioned table / Audit Log). Nothing in the catalog covers the *actual-time* axis (Effectivity / application-time period / "price valid from-to", "employment from-to"). Every temporal source treats these as two different things; an `EffectiveDated` (valid-from/valid-to, no-overlap key, as-of query parameter) trait is the most clearly missing lifecycle trait, with bitemporal = EffectiveDated + Revisioned.
- `Versioned` (optimistic concurrency token) is unrelated to versions-as-history; the naming collision with Temporal Object/SCD "versions" is a documentation risk.
- AppendOnly needs a companion for correction. The accounting literature offers exactly three options (replace, reverse, post difference); "reverse" is the one compatible with immutability. A `Reversible`/`Voidable` capability (generate a compensating record linked to the original) is missing.
- The Frappe docstatus model is a *fixed, generic* three-state machine plus an amend link, distinct from a user-defined Workflow: Draft (mutable, deletable) -> Submitted/Posted (immutable except whitelisted fields; has side effects such as ledger postings) -> Cancelled (terminal, side effects reversed) -> Amended (new draft with back-link). This is common enough in ERP to deserve either its own archetype ("Document") or a preset of Workflow, especially because it also governs the Detail children (lines are editable only while the parent is Draft).
- Approval (maker-checker) is orthogonal to the state machine: it adds the rule "the actor of transition B must differ from the actor of transition A" and records who/when. It composes as a trait on Workflow/Document/any Crud change (pending-change approval).
- Reservation is a lifecycle composed of pieces the catalog already has (Expiring for the timeout, Idempotent for confirm/cancel, a 3-state machine) but the combination — hold reduces *available* quantity of something else and release restores it — is a distinct pattern tied to a projection (available balance).
- Gap-free numbering is a lifecycle concern, not a key-generation detail: the number must be assigned inside the same transaction as the state change that makes the document official (typically on submit/post, not on draft creation), from a counter row, never from a DB sequence.
- Expiring in the catalog matches Coad's "until it expires"; "archive/deactivate" (still visible, no longer selectable — typical for Lookup rows and master data) is neither SoftDeletable nor Expiring and has no verified pattern name beyond Silverston's status patterns and Arlow's optional introduction/expiration dates on reference items.

### Gaps
- **Draft -> publish** (a published version stays live while a new draft is edited; publish swaps them; unpublish) is a CMS-originated lifecycle; no source in the assigned list was found that defines it. Unverified lead: Fowler's "Proposed Object" (listed in the eaaDev index, page not read) is the nearest named pattern.
- **Period close / locking** (no posting into a closed accounting period) — not verified; only Fowler's "mutable until booked" is sourced.
- **Approval chains** (multi-level, amount thresholds, delegation) — no primary source fetched beyond the maker-checker definition.
- PostgreSQL native temporal support: Wikipedia's SQL:2011 article still says PostgreSQL needs an extension. Unverified lead from background knowledge: PostgreSQL 18 (2025) added `WITHOUT OVERLAPS` primary/unique keys and `PERIOD` foreign keys (application-time constraints) but not system versioning; this should be checked against the PostgreSQL 18 release notes before relying on it for the ORM-config generator.
- `UPDATE/DELETE ... FOR PORTION OF` syntax was not confirmed by the Wikipedia article text.
- Pat Helland's "Life beyond Distributed Transactions" (tentative operation / confirmation / cancellation) was not read; only EIP's attribution to Helland is sourced.
- Idempotent posting to a ledger (dedupe key per posting) — no ledger-specific primary source fetched; only the generic DDD statement on recognizing duplicate events and TCC's idempotent confirm/cancel are sourced.
- Legal gap-free numbering requirements (invoice numbering laws) were not researched.

## Key Question 4: For each kind, what is the canonical real example and what rules follow (who may modify it, when, and through which parent)?

### Takeaway
The rules cluster around three questions the sources answer consistently: *through what* an object is reached and changed (root vs. member), *until when* it is mutable (always, until booked/submitted, never), and *how* an error is corrected (overwrite, new version, or compensating record). These rules, not the data shape, are what distinguish one archetype from another.

### Cited Findings
- [V] **Owned detail (Order -> OrderItem, Sale -> SaleDetail)**: reachable and modifiable only through the root; root enforces whole-aggregate invariants (e.g. total); loaded/saved/deleted with the root; transactions do not span aggregates. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf); [Fowler, DDD_Aggregate](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DDD_Aggregate.html); Coad: the moment-interval owns `addDetail` and computes its total from the details — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Independent entity (Customer, Product, Vehicle)**: has its own identity and repository; other aggregates hold only its identity. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)
- [V] **Value object (Money, date range)**: immutable, replaced as a whole, no identity, stored inside its owner. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf); [PoEAA catalog](https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/)
- [V] **Description / type (VehicleDescription, product catalog entry)**: one description to many things; supplies default values; hosts queries across all its things (e.g. how many are available). — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Knowledge-level objects (AccountabilityType, PartyType, connection rules)**: instances of these *validate* operational objects (which party types may be connected by which accountability type). — [Fowler, Accountability chapter](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf)
- [V] **Role (Cashier, Owner; Employee, Customer)**: created against a role player; carries its own assigned number and status; cross-role rules live on the player. — [Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)
- [V] **Hierarchy node (org unit)**: exactly one parent except the top; no cycles (checked when assigning the parent); values may roll down from parent to children. — [Fowler, Accountability chapter](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf). Moving a non-leaf subtree rewrites n rows under path encodings but one row under parent/child. — [Microsoft Learn: Hierarchical Data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/hierarchical-data-sql-server)
- [V] **Accounting entry / transaction**: entries are never the unit of correction; errors are fixed by replacement, reversal or difference adjustments; the balance is never edited, only derived. — [Fowler, Accounting Narrative](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/AccountingNarrative.html)
- [V] **Domain event / event log**: immutable, carries occurrence time and entry time; state is rebuilt from it. — [DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf); [Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)
- [S] **Submittable document (invoice, journal entry, stock entry in ERPNext)**: editable only in Draft; after Submit only whitelisted fields; Cancel is terminal and blocked while dependants are active; change after submit = cancel + amend (new linked document). — [Frappe docs: docstatus](https://docs.frappe.io/framework/doctypes/docstatus)
- [S] **Document chain (quotation -> sales order -> delivery -> invoice)**: each subsequent document is created *with reference to* its predecessor, copying data under configurable copy rules, and the link is recorded per item so the flow can be navigated both ways. — [SAP Help: Document Flow](https://help.sap.com/saphelp_470/helpdata/en/93/744f86546011d1a7020000e829fd11/content.htm)
- [S] **Maker-checker item (payment instruction)**: the creator cannot be the authorizer. — [Wikipedia: Maker-checker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maker-checker)
- [S] **Reservation (inventory hold, seat hold)**: created by "try", ended by confirm, cancel, or timeout; confirm/cancel idempotent. — [Oracle MicroTx docs: TCC](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/transaction-manager-for-microservices/24.4/tmmdg/tcc-transaction-model.html)
- [S] **Reference data (country, currency, status codes)**: stable value lists that must exist before master and transaction data; master/"transaction structure" data (customer, product) must exist before a transaction fires; transaction audit data tracks a transaction's progress. — [Chisholm](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/185-master-data-whatever-that-is-has-to-be-properly-managed)
- [V] **System-versioned row**: the application never writes the period columns or the history table; the engine does on every update/delete. — [Microsoft Learn: Temporal tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/tables/temporal-tables)
- [S] **Custom-attribute values (EAV)**: validation cannot be enforced by the schema and moves to application code/metadata tables. — [Wikipedia: EAV model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity%E2%80%93attribute%E2%80%93value_model)

### Inferences
- A compact rule vocabulary covers all kinds found: (1) *access path* — root (own endpoints) / through parent only / embedded; (2) *mutability window* — always / until state S / never; (3) *correction mode* — overwrite / new version / compensating record / cancel-and-amend; (4) *deletion mode* — hard / soft / with parent / never (cancel instead); (5) *who* — any authorized actor / a different actor than the creator / system only (projections, history). Each archetype in a principled catalog can be specified as one value on each of these five dimensions; traits change one dimension at a time.
- Rule (2) propagates from parent to Detail: lines are mutable exactly while the header is (verified for Frappe-style documents by the "locked after submit" rule and implied by aggregate invariants). The Detail archetype should therefore inherit the parent's mutability window automatically.
- Projection/balance entities have rule (5) = system only: no write endpoints, rebuildable; today's `ReadOnly` covers the endpoint surface but not the "derived from X, rebuild" semantics.

### Gaps
- No verified source for who may modify *reference data* (typically administrators / deployments only) or for singleton configuration rules.
- Rules for hierarchies with ordered siblings (reorder endpoint, position gaps) are not stated in any source read, beyond hierarchyid's ability to generate a value between two siblings.

## Key Question 5: Which distinctions do several independent authors agree on (strong evidence the category is fundamental)?

### Takeaway
Ten distinctions are each attested by three or more independent traditions (OO analysis, DDD, data modeling/MDM, dimensional modeling, database standards, ERP practice). Where the catalog lacks a counterpart to one of these, the omission is very likely real rather than a matter of taste.

### Cited Findings
- **(1) Thing vs. its type/description** — Coad blue vs. green [V] ([Coad ch.1](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/colorModeling/jmcuch01.pdf)); Fowler knowledge vs. operational level, thing/thing-type, type object [V] ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/apsupp/accountability.pdf)); Arlow & Neustadt Product pattern with pleomorphs such as IdenticalProduct [V for existence] ([Arlow ch.1](https://www.pearsonhighered.com/assets/samplechapter/0/3/2/1/032111230X.pdf)); MDM reference data as its own layer [S] ([Chisholm](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/185-master-data-whatever-that-is-has-to-be-properly-managed)).

- **(6) Immutable record of what happened + correction by compensation** — DDD domain events immutable [V]; Fowler Event Sourcing, Audit Log, Reversal/Difference Adjustment [V]; Kimball SCD type 0 and transaction facts [V/S]; Frappe cancel-and-amend instead of editing submitted documents [S].
- **(7) Two independent time axes** — Fowler actual vs. record time [V] ([timeNarrative](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/timeNarrative.html)); Snodgrass valid vs. transaction time (as cited by Fowler) [V]; SQL:2011 application time vs. system time [S] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL:2011)); DDD domain event's two timestamps (occurred vs. entered) [V] ([DDD Reference](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf)); Kimball SCD types as the warehouse-side version taxonomy [V].
- **(8) Source-of-truth records vs. derived state** — Fowler balance from entries, state from events [V]; CQRS read model [V] ([Fowler, CQRS](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/CQRS.html)); Kimball periodic and accumulating snapshots [S].
- **(9) Hierarchy as a special relationship with its own rules and storage** — Fowler Organization Hierarchy [V]; Karwin's four tree models [S]; PostgreSQL ltree [V]; SQL Server hierarchyid [V]; Rails self joins [V].
- **(10) Chains of business events/documents with explicit predecessor/successor links** — Coad PriorMI/NextMI and mi_generateNext [V]; SAP document flow and copy control [S]; Frappe `amended_from` [S].
- **(11) Subtype families and their three table mappings; polymorphic reference** — PoEAA [V]; Rails STI / delegated types / polymorphic associations [V]; Fowler Party as supertype [V]; Silverston subtypes/supertypes convention [S].
- **(12) An archetype is a soft template with optional parts, not a base class** — Coad ("more or less follow"; no inheritance) [V]; Arlow & Neustadt (optional `«o»` features, pattern configuration, pleomorphism) [V].

### Inferences
- Distinctions (1), (2), (5), (7-application-time), (8), (9), (10), (11) have no direct counterpart in the current catalog; (3), (4), (6) are partially covered (Workflow/Crud, Detail, AppendOnly). These are the strongest candidates for additions, in roughly this order of generator impact: value object/embedded (affects DTO + ORM config of nearly every entity), hierarchy trait, effective dating, type-instance reference with defaults, subtype family, document lifecycle with posting/reversal and predecessor links, projection/balance, role.
- Agreement (12) validates the overall archetype + trait design and suggests the generator should model "optional member not emitted" separately from "nullable member".
- Disagreement worth noting: Coad treats *role* as one of only four fundamental archetypes, whereas DDD has no role concept at all (a role is just another entity or a separate bounded-context view of the same person). So "Role" is fundamental in the OO-analysis/data-modeling tradition but optional in DDD; it could be a trait (`RoleOf<Party>`: 1:0..1 extension sharing identity) rather than an archetype.
- Disagreement on immutability strictness: Fowler lists Replacement Adjustment (delete and recreate) as a legitimate option and says entries may be mutable until booked, whereas event-sourcing/DDD treat events as immutable. A framework should make "immutable from state S" configurable rather than assume insert-only.

### Gaps
- Counting "independent" agreement is limited by what could be read: Silverston and Hay were seen only at TOC level, and Arlow & Neustadt only in chapter 1, so their agreement on (1), (2) and (4) is weaker evidence here than it would be with full text.
- No source was found that proposes a single unified taxonomy spanning structure, relationships and lifecycle; the synthesis tables below are this researcher's consolidation, not a published classification.
